Multiclass Classification of News Articles

September 2024 · 5 min read

In a Nutshell

This project explores the use of machine learning techniques to automatically categorize large sets of textual data. Using a diverse dataset of news articles, various preprocessing steps, feature engineering techniques, and classification models were tested to identify the best approach. The study addresses key challenges such as class imbalance and evaluates different models to optimize performance.

Why Should You Care About This Project?

Consider the following problems:

  • A customer support team is flooded with tickets, each needing to be routed to the appropriate department — billing, technical issues, or product inquiries.
  • An e-commerce platform has thousands of new products being added daily, each needing to be categorized accurately.
  • A legal firm is struggling to organize thousands of case files spread across multiple areas of law.

Each of these problems have one thing in common — lots of textual data that needs to be analyzed and categorized. Multiclass text classification offers a solution. The business potential goes beyond just one industry: in customer service, healthcare, law, e-commerce, and many other fields, automating the classification of large volumes of unstructured text data is crucial.

About the Dataset

The dataset consists of over 200,000 news articles from the Huffington Post, spanning from 2012 to 2022. Each article includes a headline, a short description, and a predefined category, with 42 unique categories such as 'Politics,' 'Sports,' and 'Arts.' The dataset is inherently unstructured and exhibits class imbalance, with certain categories having significantly more samples than others.

Exploratory Data Analysis

After looking at the distribution of articles across categories, several observations emerged: 'STYLE' could be a subset of 'STYLE & BEAUTY', 'CULTURE & ARTS', 'ARTS & CULTURE', and 'ARTS' categories are very close in meaning and count, 'WORLDPOST' could be the same as 'THE WORLDPOST', and 'TASTE' could be a subcategory of 'FOOD & DRINK'.

It is worthwhile to examine if the above categories can be combined as it would help with reducing the imbalance and have cleaner categories.

Preparing the Textual Data

Text preprocessing is essential because, unlike humans who can naturally infer meaning and context, computers require structured data. Raw text is unstructured, with variations in capitalization, punctuation, and word forms. Preprocessing transforms text into a standardized format, converting it into numerical representations that machines can interpret.

Feature Engineering and Model Selection

Two primary methods of feature engineering were used: sparse vector representations (CountVectorizer, TF-IDF) that work well with traditional ML models like Naive Bayes and SVM, and dense embeddings (Word2Vec) that capture semantic meaning for models like deep neural networks.

Model Evaluation and Performance

The dataset was split 80/20 for training and testing. Preliminary results were promising, with Naive Bayes showing strong performance on sparse vector representations, while the non-linear SVM performed best with dense embeddings.

Conclusion

This project demonstrates the power of machine learning in categorizing large sets of textual data. By addressing challenges like high dimensionality, class imbalance, and capturing contextual meaning, the model was steadily improved through iterative steps. The results highlight the potential of multiclass classification across various industries, showing that thoughtful data preparation and continuous refinement are key to building effective models.
