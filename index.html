01 · About

I got into data the way some people get into detective novels — I liked the feeling of laying every fact on the table and figuring out what it was actually telling me. That instinct is still what drives the work, it's just the scale that's changed: from watershed simulations at UIUC to systems now processing contracts worth billions of dollars at CVS Health.

What I actually spend my time on is the unglamorous, high-leverage part of data work — profiling a slow Spark job until I find the one join that's costing eight hours, migrating tables to Unity Catalog so PII stays where it should, building validation frameworks so an analyst trusts a number without re-checking it by hand. Good infrastructure is judged by how little anyone has to think about it.

Outside of pipelines: I've led teams of junior engineers, presented recommendations to non-technical stakeholders, and I'm increasingly interested in where AI tooling fits into the data engineering SDLC — not as a novelty, but as another layer of the stack worth engineering properly.
